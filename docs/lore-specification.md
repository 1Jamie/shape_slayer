# Lore & Story System - Complete Specification

## Overview

The lore system treats **cards as memories** - fragments of a forgotten past that players recover and repair through mastery progression. The narrative centers around a time loop, the Nexus (a fragment of the old kingdom), and the four troublemakers who are trapped in an eternal cycle of memory recovery and kingdom building.

---

## Part 1: The Core Narrative

### 1.1 The World Before

**The Kingdom/Empire:**
- A thriving kingdom existed before the Sorcerer's arrival
- It was a place of magic, diversity, culture, and life
- The kingdom had its own people, history, and traditions
- The four troublemakers were important figures in the kingdom (exact roles left ambiguous)

**The Four Troublemakers:**
- **Blue (Square/Warrior)**: The historian. Remembers the kingdom, the empire, the Sorcerer's arrival. Holds the most complete memories of the past.
- **Pink (Triangle/Rogue)**: The innocent. Asks questions, doesn't remember much. Represents curiosity and the desire to understand.
- **Red (Pentagon/Tank)**: The rebel. Remembers being a troublemaker. Represents defiance and resistance to order.
- **Purple (Hexagon/Mage)**: The conflicted. Defensive about magic users, but also proud. Represents the complexity of identity.

### 1.2 The Sorcerer's Arrival

**Initial Appearance:**
- A Sorcerer claiming to be an "apostle of the god of order" arrived
- At first, he seemed helpful - solving problems, stopping conflicts
- He claimed to be bringing order to the world
- The kingdom welcomed him, thinking he was a savior

**The Truth:**
- His true goal was to remove everything that caused "disorder"
- He began systematically erasing memories, identities, and the kingdom itself
- He reduced everything to basic geometric shapes
- Those who resisted became "troublemakers" - the four players

### 1.3 The Order's Victory

**The Transformation:**
- The Sorcerer removed everything he believed caused disorder
- Memories were drained, identities erased
- The kingdom was consumed, reduced to geometric shapes
- Most shapes became mindless, only attacking when they see the players

**The Geometric World:**
- The world after the Order's victory
- Everything is reduced to geometric shapes
- The world is fragmented, rooms shift and change
- Memories exist as fragments scattered throughout (cards)
- The world is hostile, but also holds the key to recovery

### 1.4 The Time Loop

**The Bootstrap Paradox:**
- The time loop exists, but its origin is unknown (bootstrap paradox)
- The loop exists because it was cast, but who cast it is lost to time
- **Possible Casters** (left ambiguous):
  - The kingdom's last mages/scholars (sacrificed themselves)
  - A future version of the four (bootstrap paradox)
  - An unknown ally or resistance group
  - The kingdom itself (a failsafe spell that activated automatically)
  - The Sorcerer (unintended consequence of their magic)
  - Something else entirely (leave it open)

**The Echo's Mechanism:**
- The echo was created as a last resort to preserve the kingdom's memory
- It anchors to the Nexus (a fragment of the old kingdom)
- The four are bound to the Nexus as the "preserved ones"
- The echo resets their personal memories each cycle
- But the Nexus remembers everything they recover (it's outside the reset)

**The Echo's Purpose:**
- **Primary Purpose**: To preserve the kingdom's memory forever, frozen in time
- **Secondary Purpose**: To give the four infinite chances to recover fragments from the past
- **True Nature**: An eternal snapshot of what was, from which they can recover memories, rebuild and learn from

**Why They Don't Die:**
- The loop preserves their existence - they can't truly die while the loop exists
- When they "die" in the geometric world, the Nexus pulls them back
- They're bound to the Nexus, which exists outside the loop's reset
- The loop is both protection and preservation

### 1.5 The Nexus

**What It Is:**
- A fragment of the old kingdom, preserved in the void between the loop and the geometric world
- The anchor point of the time loop - it exists outside the loop's reset
- Starts as a small, empty void with just the portal
- Grows with each cycle, becoming a new kingdom

**Why It's Special:**
- It's the only place that remembers across cycles
- It stores recovered memories (cards), resources, and people
- It's the foundation for building a new kingdom
- It's their home, their sanctuary, their purpose

**The Void:**
- The space between the loop and the geometric world
- Infinite potential, but starts empty
- They're building a new kingdom in the void, piece by piece
- The void is both prison and canvas

### 1.6 The Echo and Memory Recovery

**What They Recover From the Echo:**
- **Memories (Cards)**: Pieces of the kingdom's history, recovered from the echo's frozen moments
- **Shards**: Crystallized essence, remnants of the old world preserved in the echo
- **People (NPCs)**: Echoes of the old kingdom, rescued from the geometric world
- **Materials**: Fragments of the past, preserved in the echo's snapshot
- **Knowledge**: Understanding unlocked through exploration of the echo's preserved moments

**How It Works:**
- Each cycle through the echo is a journey into the past
- They explore the frozen moments, recover fragments, rescue echoes of people
- When they "die," they return to the Nexus with what they've recovered
- The Nexus stores everything permanently (meta-progression)
- Each cycle, the Nexus grows - new NPCs, buildings, areas built from recovered fragments

**The Echo's True Nature:**
- The echo isn't a prison - it's a preserved memory of what was
- Each cycle is an opportunity to recover more from the past
- The echo becomes a gift, not a curse - a forever memory to draw from
- They're not trapped - they're recovering and rebuilding from the echo's preserved moments

## Part 2: The Endings

### 2.1 Ending 1: Breaking the Loop (The "Wrong" Choice)

**How to Achieve:**
- After defeating the final boss, a choice appears
- Option: "Break the Loop" - Requires finding a specific item/memory
- The choice is presented as "freedom" but feels ominous
- The game warns the player that breaking the loop will destroy the Nexus

**What Happens:**
- They break the loop
- The Nexus collapses (it was anchored to the loop)
- The geometric world returns to normal (the Order wins completely)
- They're free, but everything they built is gone
- The kingdom's memory is lost forever
- They're alone in a world that's already been consumed

**The Reveal Dialog:**
```
Blue: We can break it. We can escape.
Red: Finally! Freedom!
Purple: But... the Nexus. Everything we've built.
Blue: It will be destroyed. But we'll be free.
Pink: Free to do what? The world is gone. The kingdom is gone.
Blue: We'll be free.
[They break the loop]
[The Nexus collapses]
[They're alone in the geometric world]
Blue: What have we done?
Red: We're free. That's what we wanted, right?
Purple: Free to be alone. Free to watch the world die.
Pink: The kingdom... it's gone. Forever.
Blue: We made a mistake.
```

**Tone:**
- Somber, tragic
- They realize they made a mistake
- Freedom without purpose is meaningless
- The ending is bittersweet - they're free, but they've lost everything

**Achievement:**
- "The Price of Freedom" - Break the loop and lose everything

**Gameplay Impact:**
- The Nexus is destroyed
- All progress is saved separately as a "broken loop" save
- The game can still be played, but the Nexus is gone. you keep playing until you die. Though this time when you die you actually die and the game ends. takes you back to the main menu. there are now two save files. one that is broken and a copy that puts them back in the nexus like they didnt break the loop.
- New runs starts from a previous save.

### 2.2 Ending 2: Staying in the Loop (The "True" Ending)

**How to Achieve:**
- After defeating the final boss, choose "Continue the Loop"
- Requires building the Nexus to a certain level (e.g., 50+ NPCs, all buildings unlocked, certain achievements)
- The choice is presented as "acceptance" and feels right
- The game encourages this choice through narrative and gameplay

**What Happens:**
- They accept their role as eternal guardians
- The Nexus continues to grow
- They build a new kingdom in the void
- The old kingdom's memory is preserved forever
- They find peace and purpose
- The echo becomes a gift, not a curse
- **Final Milestone**: Restore the Queen, their close friend and the kingdom's last ruler

**The Reveal Dialog:**
```
Blue: We can break it. But should we?
Red: What do you mean? We've been fighting to escape!
Purple: But look at what we've built. The Nexus. The people. The kingdom.
Pink: It's beautiful. It's ours.
Blue: The loop isn't a prison. It's a gift. The kingdom gave us eternity. Not to suffer, but to build.
Red: So we stay?
Blue: We stay. We continue. We build.
Purple: And the old kingdom?
Blue: Preserved. Forever. In us. In the Nexus. In every memory we've recovered.
Pink: So we're not trapped. We're home.
Blue: Exactly. We're home.
[The Nexus expands, becomes a full kingdom]
[They find peace]
```

**Tone:**
- Hopeful, beautiful
- They find meaning in their eternal role
- The loop becomes a gift, not a curse
- The ending is satisfying - they've found their purpose

**Achievement:**
- "Eternal Kingdom" - Build the Nexus into a kingdom and accept your role
- "The Queen's Return" - Restore the Queen and complete the kingdom's restoration (ultimate achievement)

**Gameplay Impact:**
- The Nexus continues to grow
- New content unlocks (post-game expansion)
- Players can continue building the Nexus indefinitely
- The echo becomes the source for continued recovery and building
- **Final Unlock**: Restore the Queen (requires specific conditions)

## Part 3: The Nexus Kingdom System

### 3.1 Nexus Expansion

**Starting State:**
- Empty void with just the portal
- No NPCs, no buildings, no structures
- Minimal, desolate, but full of potential

**Growth Mechanism:**
- Each cycle, the Nexus grows
- New NPCs appear (rescued from the geometric world)
- New buildings unlock (built from resources)
- New areas open up (expanding the void)
- The Nexus becomes a living, breathing kingdom

**Expansion Triggers:**
- **NPCs**: Defeating certain enemies, recovering specific memories, building certain structures
- **Buildings**: Gathering resources, unlocking achievements, reaching milestones
- **Areas**: Reaching certain NPC/building counts, completing quests, unlocking achievements
- **The Queen**: Requires all major memories recovered, Nexus fully built, true ending achieved (see Queen's Restoration section)

### 3.2 NPCs and People

**How NPCs Appear:**
- **Rescued from the Echo**: Defeating certain enemies or bosses rescues NPCs from the echo
- **Memory Recovery**: Recovering specific memories unlocks NPCs
- **Building Unlocks**: Building certain structures attracts NPCs
- **Achievement Rewards**: Completing achievements brings NPCs
- **The Queen**: Restored only after meeting all conditions (see Queen's Restoration section)

**Types of NPCs:**
- **Citizens**: Basic NPCs who populate the Nexus (provide atmosphere, dialog)
- **Merchants**: Sell items, provide upgrade services
- **Guards**: Protect the Nexus (cosmetic/atmospheric)
- **Scholars**: Provide lore, hints, story information
- **Artisans**: Help with customization, building, crafting
- **Leaders**: Special NPCs who unlock new features, provide quests
- **The Queen**: The ultimate NPC - the kingdom's last ruler, restored as the final milestone

**NPC Characteristics:**
- Each NPC has a name, personality, and dialog
- They remember previous cycles (they're part of the Nexus)
- They contribute to the kingdom's growth
- They provide services, quests, and story content

**NPC Progression:**
- Start with 0 NPCs (empty void)
- Each cycle, more NPCs appear
- NPCs have relationships, stories, and purposes
- They make the Nexus feel alive and populated

### 3.3 Buildings and Structures

**Building Types:**
1. **Housing** - Homes for NPCs (unlocks more NPC capacity)
2. **Marketplace** - Trading, merchants, commerce
3. **Library** - Lore, codex, memory storage
4. **Training Grounds** - Practice, tutorials, combat training
5. **Monument** - Commemorates achievements, honors the old kingdom
6. **Garden** - Decorative, peaceful area
7. **Workshop** - Customization, crafting, building
8. **Temple** - Spiritual, memory-focused, honors the old kingdom
9. **Wall** - Defensive structures (cosmetic, but provides atmosphere)
10. **Portal Chamber** - The original portal, enhanced and expanded

**Building Unlocks:**
- **Resources**: Built from shards, materials from runs
- **Requirements**: Certain NPCs, achievements, memories
- **Progression**: Buildings unlock in tiers (basic → advanced → legendary)

**Building Placement:**
- Players can place buildings anywhere in the Nexus
- Customizable layout, spacing, arrangement
- Buildings can be moved, upgraded, decorated
- Placement affects NPC behavior and Nexus aesthetics

### 3.4 Areas and Zones

**Nexus Zones:**
1. **The Core** - The original portal area (always present)
2. **The Residential District** - Housing, NPCs, living spaces
3. **The Market District** - Commerce, trading, merchants
4. **The Memory Quarter** - Library, monuments, lore
5. **The Training Grounds** - Practice, combat, tutorials
6. **The Gardens** - Peaceful, decorative, serene
7. **The Outer Ring** - Expansion area, customizable, new construction

**Zone Unlocking:**
- Zones unlock as the Nexus grows
- Each zone has a theme and purpose
- Players can customize each zone's layout
- Zones provide structure and organization

### 3.5 Customization System

**Layout Customization:**
- **Drag and Drop**: Move buildings, NPCs, decorations
- **Grid-Based**: Optional grid for alignment
- **Free Placement**: Place items anywhere
- **Snap to Grid**: Toggle for precise placement

**Customization Options:**
- **Building Placement**: Where buildings go
- **Path Layout**: Create paths between areas
- **Decoration Placement**: Add trees, statues, lights, fountains
- **Zone Themes**: Choose themes for each zone
- **Color Schemes**: Customize colors, materials, aesthetics

**Visual Customization:**
- **Building Styles**: Geometric, Kingdom, Modern, Hybrid
- **Materials**: Stone, Crystal, Memory, Void
- **Decorations**: Statues, Fountains, Gardens, Lights, Monuments

**Save/Load Layouts:**
- Save multiple layout presets
- Share layouts with other players (optional)
- Reset to default if desired
- Export/import layouts

### 3.6 Recovering From the Echo

**What You Recover From the Echo Each Cycle:**
- **Memories (Cards)**: Pieces of the kingdom's history, recovered from the echo's frozen moments, stored in the Library
- **Shards**: Crystallized essence, remnants of the old world preserved in the echo, used to build structures
- **Materials**: Fragments of the past (Stone, Crystal, Memory Fragments, Essence), preserved in the echo's snapshot
- **People (NPCs)**: Echoes of the old kingdom, rescued from the geometric world, appear after certain conditions
- **Knowledge**: Understanding unlocked through exploration of the echo's preserved moments, unlocks new buildings and features

**Resource Sources:**
- **Enemies**: Drop shards, materials, sometimes NPCs
- **Bosses**: Drop rare materials, guaranteed NPCs, special items
- **Elite Rooms**: Drop bonus resources, rare materials
- **Hidden Rooms**: Drop unique resources, special NPCs, lore items
- **Achievements**: Unlock knowledge, special buildings, NPCs

**Resource Management:**
- Resources are stored in the Nexus permanently
- Resources are used to build structures, upgrade buildings
- Resources accumulate across cycles (meta-progression)
- Resource caps may exist to prevent infinite accumulation

### 3.7 The Queen's Restoration

**Who Is The Queen:**
- The kingdom's last ruler before the Order's victory
- An extremely close and personal friend of the four troublemakers
- Preserved in the echo, waiting to be restored
- The ultimate symbol of the kingdom's restoration

**Restoration Requirements:**
- **All Major Memories Recovered**: Specific key cards must be unlocked and mastered
- **Nexus Fully Built**: All major buildings constructed, all zones unlocked
- **A lot of NPCs**: The Nexus must be a thriving community
- **True Ending Achieved**: Must have chosen to stay in the echo (not break it)
- **Specific Achievements**: Complete certain story-related achievements
- **Post-Game Progression**: Continue building after the true ending

**What Happens When Restored:**
- The Queen appears in the Nexus as a special NPC
- She provides unique dialog, story closure, and guidance
- She helps guide the Nexus's continued growth
- She represents the completion of the kingdom's restoration
- The four find true peace and closure with their friend restored

**The Queen's Role:**
- Provides closure to the story
- Represents the ultimate success of rebuilding
- Guides the Nexus's continued evolution
- Serves as a reminder of what was lost and what was gained
- Symbolizes the friendship and bonds that survived the Order's destruction

## Part 4: Card Flavor Text System

### 4.1 Memory Progression Structure

Each card's flavor text across quality bands tells a **progressive memory story**. Each quality band builds upon the previous one, revealing more detail as the memory is repaired.

**Format Rules:**
1. **White (Common)**: Fragmentary, vague memory. Just a feeling or basic concept.
2. **Green (Uncommon)**: Memory becomes clearer. A specific detail emerges.
3. **Blue (Rare)**: More context appears. The memory connects to something larger.
4. **Purple (Epic)**: The full memory emerges. Clear understanding of what it represents.
5. **Orange (Legendary)**: The complete, restored memory. Full context and significance revealed.

**Example Structure (Precision Card):**

**White**: "A fragment. Something about... accuracy? Hitting true."
- Fragment: Just a feeling about precision

**Green**: "I remember training. Hours spent perfecting aim. The satisfaction of a perfect strike."
- Detail: It's about training, practice

**Blue**: "The kingdom's archers. They could hit a target from impossible distances. I was one of them."
- Context: It's about being an archer in the kingdom

**Purple**: "We defended the walls. Every arrow found its mark. The Sorcerer's forces learned to fear our precision."
- Understanding: It's about defending the kingdom, their role

**Orange**: "I was the Master Archer. My arrows never missed. Not even the Sorcerer's magic could deflect them."
- Complete Memory: Their identity, their power, their significance

**Legendary Flavor**: "In the final battle, I pierced the Sorcerer's barrier. One arrow, one moment of perfect precision, and the Order cracked. We lost, but I proved it could be broken."
- Expanded Context: Their role in the final battle, the significance of this memory

### 4.2 Garbled Text Mechanic: Upgrading Past Mastery

**Critical Design Rule**: You cannot force memories by upgrading cards past your mastery level.

**How It Works:**
1. **In-Run Upgrades Can Exceed Mastery**: Players can upgrade cards in-hand beyond their current mastery level (subject to room-based upgrade limits)
2. **Garbled Text Display**: If a card's quality band exceeds the player's mastery level, the flavor text displays as **garbled text** instead of the actual memory
3. **Mastery Unlocks Understanding**: When you unlock mastery for a quality band, you can now "read" that memory

**Example Scenario:**
- Player has Precision at Mastery 2 (Blue unlocked)
- In Room 15, player upgrades Precision from Blue → Purple using an upgrade reward
- Result: Card functions as Purple (mechanical benefits work), but flavor text shows garbled
- After Unlocking Mastery 3: Same card now shows full Purple flavor text

**Garbled Text Format:**
- `"#@$%&*! ~memory~ corrupted... @#$%&*"`
- `"Static... fragments... cannot... parse..."`
- `"The memory resists... not yet understood..."`
- `"Geometric noise... meaning lost..."`
- `"Order interference... memory blocked..."`
- `"Fragments scattered... pattern incomplete..."`

**Quality Band to Mastery Mapping:**
- White = Mastery 0 (always readable, starter cards)
- Green = Mastery 1
- Blue = Mastery 2
- Purple = Mastery 3
- Orange = Mastery 4

**Mastery 5 Special Case:**
- Mastery 5 doesn't unlock a new quality band
- It provides bonuses to existing quality bands
- Legendary flavor text (orange only) is always readable if you have the card at orange quality
- Mastery 5 may unlock additional narrative content (special dialog, codex entries)

### 4.3 Flavor Text Writing Guidelines

1. **Start vague, end specific**: Each band should reveal more concrete information
2. **Build narrative**: Connect to the larger story of the old world
3. **Character voice**: Consider which character's memory this might be (Blue's historical knowledge, Red's combat memories, etc.)
4. **Thematic consistency**: Cards in the same family should have related memories
5. **Mystery preservation**: Don't reveal everything - leave room for discovery

### 4.4 Card Family Memory Themes

- **Offense Cards**: Memories of combat, battle, conflict, the war against the Sorcerer
- **Defense Cards**: Memories of protection, safety, the kingdom's defenses
- **Mobility Cards**: Memories of travel, exploration, the world before
- **Ability Cards**: Memories of special powers, magic, the old ways
- **Economy Cards**: Memories of trade, resources, the kingdom's wealth

## Part 5: Dialog System

### 5.1 Progression-Based Dialog

Characters speak at specific progression milestones, remembering more as they progress further.

**Dialog Triggers:**
- **Room Milestones**: Every 5 rooms (5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, etc.)
- **Boss Defeats**: After defeating each boss
- **First Time Events**: First time reaching certain rooms, first boss, etc.
- **Near-Death Experiences**: When HP drops below 20% (rare dialog)
- **Card Unlocks**: When unlocking certain significant cards
- **Mastery Milestones**: When reaching Mastery 5 on a card (rare, significant dialog)
- **Nexus Milestones**: When the Nexus reaches certain growth levels

**Dialog Structure:**
Each dialog entry should:
1. **Identify the speaker** (Blue, Pink, Red, Purple)
2. **Be contextually relevant** to the progression point
3. **Reveal new information** about the world or their past
4. **Maintain character voice** (Blue = historian, Pink = curious, Red = defiant, Purple = conflicted)
5. **Build toward the goal**: Understanding the loop, building the Nexus, finding purpose

## Part 6: Hidden Lore Rooms

### 6.1 Concept

Special rooms that appear rarely, containing:
- **Lore fragments**: Pieces of the story
- **Memory cards**: Unique cards with special lore text
- **Collectibles**: Items that unlock additional story content
- **Dialog triggers**: Significant conversations
- **NPCs**: Special NPCs that can be rescued

### 6.2 Room Types

**Memory Vaults:**
- **Rarity**: Very rare (1-2% chance to replace a normal room)
- **Appearance**: Distinctive visual (glowing, different geometry)
- **Contents**: 
  - A special "Memory Card" (unique card, cannot be found elsewhere)
  - Significant dialog
  - Lore text reveal
  - Sometimes an NPC

**Echo Chambers:**
- **Rarity**: Rare (3-5% chance)
- **Appearance**: Echoing, reverberating visual effects
- **Contents**:
  - Dialog from the past (ghostly voices)
  - No combat, just story
  - Unlocks a lore entry in the codex
  - Reveals information about the loop, the kingdom, or the Sorcerer

**Fragment Shrines:**
- **Rarity**: Uncommon (5-8% chance)
- **Appearance**: Geometric shrine structure
- **Contents**:
  - Guaranteed high-quality card (Blue+)
  - Special flavor text reveal
  - Brief dialog
  - Sometimes materials or shards

**The Library:**

- **Mysterious but hopeful**: The world is broken, but there's a way to fix it
- **Character-driven**: Each character has a distinct voice
- **Gradual revelation**: Don't reveal everything at once
- **Emotional resonance**: Connect to themes of memory, identity, resistance

### 12.2 Character Voices

**Blue (Warrior)**:
- Formal, historical
- Uses complete sentences
- References the past with certainty
- "I remember..." is a common phrase

**Pink (Rogue)**:
- Questions everything
- Simple, direct language
- Represents innocence and curiosity
- "What's...?" is a common phrase

**Red (Tank)**:
- Defiant, aggressive
- Short, punchy sentences
- Focuses on action, not reflection
- "We should..." or "Let's..." are common phrases

**Purple (Mage)**:
- Defensive, proud
- Complex sentences about magic
- Struggles with identity
- "But magic..." or "I'm not..." are common phrases
